Cybernetics with Chinese Characteristics

Exhibition concept · Chinese cybernetic art

回路Modern Chinese Cybernetics

huílù · a circuit, a loop, a route that comes back

Curatorial statement

In 1960s and 70s China, cybernetics offered a distinctly political vision of the relationship between humans and machines. The “Cyber Great Leap Forward” imagined technology not simply as a force of automation, but as part of a feedback loop in which workers and machines continually shaped one another. Workers were expected to contribute their knowledge and creativity to technological development, while technology would in turn expand human capabilities. This vision positioned cybernetics as a collective project: technology was supposed to increase human agency rather than replace it or concentrate it in a technical elite.


Contemporary Chinese and Chinese-diasporic artists revisit this relationship under very different technological conditions. Their works ask what happens when humans no longer simply use machines, but participate in systems that observe, learn from, respond to, and sometimes control them. From Miao Ying’s exploration of internet censorship and digital platforms, to Sougwen Chung’s collaborations with learning machines, Vivian Xu’s feedback between radiation, machinery, and material decay, and Cao Fei’s workers negotiating industrial production with personal expression, these artists reveal technology as a system of relationships rather than a neutral tool. Together, the works reconsider the promise of cybernetics: when humans and machines enter a feedback loop, who is actually shaping whom?

Room I

Modern Chinese Society through Cybernetic Art

In these works the artists examine modern Chinese society through cybernetic art. Reevaluating Chinese traditions, internet culture and industrial labor, they comment on societal issues and try to imagine a path forward for China.

Past Matchmaking Norms and Their Influence

Liu Dao (六岛) · LED interactive installation, mixed media

Liu Dao (六岛), the in-house art collective at Shanghai’s island6 Arts Center, produces LED light sculptures and interactive installations that bridge traditional Chinese visual motifs with cutting-edge technology. This work engages with the persistence of matchmaking customs in contemporary China, where parents gather in public parks to advertise their unmarried children — a practice that has migrated from village matchmakers to urban “marriage markets.” The collective renders this social ritual through illuminated, sensor-driven surfaces that respond to the presence of viewers.

ArtistLiu Dao is a Shanghai-based collective founded in 2006, known for interactive LED installations that blend traditional Chinese imagery with new media.

Hardcore Digital Detox

Miao Ying · 2018 · Browser-based net art, interactive web collage

Miao Ying’s Hardcore Digital Detox parodies the digital wellness industry by presenting the Chinese internet — with its blocks on Google, Facebook, and other services — as a relaxing online retreat. Commissioned by M+ Museum Hong Kong as the first work in their digital commissions series, the piece takes the form of a long scrolling webpage with eleven slides of bilingual text, rendered in a deliberately ugly late-1990s/early-2000s web aesthetic: low-resolution GIFs, PowerPoint-style transitions, and clashing colors. The work is a playful yet pointed reflection on global capitalism, online propaganda, and media democracy.

ArtistMiao Ying (b. 1985, Shanghai) is a Chinese artist known for net art and writing that explores internet censorship and digital culture.

Whose Utopia?

Cao Fei · 2006 · Video (color, sound), 20:20 min

Shot during a six-month residency at the OSRAM lighting factory in the Pearl River Delta, Whose Utopia? consists of three parts and illuminates the human workforce behind one of the world’s largest light bulb manufacturers. Cao Fei documented the factory’s production processes and collaborated with workers to stage performances — dancing, playing guitar, wearing costumes — within the facility. Her aim was to “release the workers from a standardized notion of productivity,” transforming a space of highly controlled movement into one of play and self-expression.

ArtistCao Fei (b. 1978, Guangzhou) is a multimedia artist whose work explores China’s rapid social and cultural transformation.

Room II

Cybernetics in Today's Human-technology Interactions

In these works, technology becomes more than a tool: it learns from the human hand, registers invisible processes, moves through mathematical systems, and carries fragments of the body beyond its limits. A robot draws from an artist’s memory; radiation becomes sound as a machine records its own decay; sculpture turns computation and movement into physical form; a tooth is sent into space as a proxy for its owner. Across these encounters, the boundary between human and machine, living and nonliving, becomes unstable. Together, the works revisit cybernetics not simply as the control of machines, but as a way of understanding how humans and technological systems shape, remember, and transform one another.

MEMORY: Drawing Operations Unit: Gen 2

Sougwen Chung · 2015 onward · Robotic arms, drawing, performance

Sougwen Chung draws beside custom robotic arms, named D.O.U.G. (Drawing Operations Unit: Generation 2), that learn from her marks and answer in their own. Using machine learning to classify archives of her own drawings, Chung trains the robot to collaborate rather than merely replicate. Each performance becomes a conversation between a hand and a machine, exploring the mark-made-by-hand and the mark-made-by-machine as a way to understand the dynamics of humans and systems.

ArtistSougwen Chung is a Chinese-Canadian artist and researcher known for pioneering human–machine collaboration in drawing and performance.

Cybernetic Duchamp

London Tsai · Kinetic sculpture, interactive cybernetic system

London Tsai’s Cybernetic Duchamp responds to Marcel Duchamp’s Bicycle Wheel (1913), a conceptual anchor of contemporary art that suggests movement but stands completely still. Tsai, who grew up encountering the Duchamp piece at MoMA, decided to make not merely a kinetic version but a cybernetic one — a bicycle wheel with interactive chaotic instability. The work is an “instabile,” an unstable mobile that foregrounds the contrast between Duchamp’s static readymade and a system that actively responds to its environment.

ArtistLondon Tsai is a Brooklyn-based artist working in kinetic and cybernetic sculpture.

Living Distance

Xin Liu · 2019–2020 · Two-channel video, VR installation, performance

Living Distance is a three-part project centered on the fantastical journey of Xin Liu’s wisdom tooth, which traveled aboard the International Space Station before returning to Earth. Carried by a crystalline robotic sculpture engineered by the artist (EBIFA — Everything Beautiful Is Far Away), the tooth metaphorically becomes a newborn entity as it enters infinite darkness. The work comprises a performance conducted in outer space, a two-channel video installation mixing documentary footage with dreamlike imagery, and a virtual reality experience that allows viewers to follow the tooth’s journey from a first-hand perspective.

ArtistXin Liu (b. 1991, Xinjiang) is an artist and engineer whose work explores the intersections of technology, nature, and human experience.

Horologic Hysteros

Vivian Xu & Benjamin Bacon · 2026 · Mixed-media installation: electronics, tape machines, magnetic tape, wood, stainless steel

Horologic Hysteros is the second installment in the Horologic Series, following Horologic Solum. The installation constructs a soundscape of industrial decay through recursive signal transfer: Geiger counter sensors capture radioactive emissions in the exhibition space, translating invisible environmental radiation into a foundational pulse; this signal feeds into a three-layer tape loop system, each layer cycling at its own interval; the tempo is not composed but extracted from the frequency of radioactive decay events; and as the tape loops, the physical degradation of the medium becomes audible, with each pass sampling the previous and accumulating generations of signal degradation. The machine ultimately inscribes its own history on paper. The work was commissioned by the Power Station of Art for the Techno Worlds: Final Sampling exhibition.

ArtistVivian Xu is a media artist and Assistant Professor at Duke Kunshan University whose work explores bio and electronic media.

Room III

Cybernetics in the Age of AI

Artificial intelligence transforms cybernetics from a science of steering into a science of learning systems that generate, imagine, and sometimes exceed their makers. These works explore what happens when algorithms become creative collaborators, when digital avatars carry the self through cycles of reincarnation, and when machine perception mediates our relationship to the world. The feedback loop here is not only technical but existential: as AI systems learn from us, they also reshape what it means to be human.

I've Seen You Cry in the Rain

Chen Baoyang · AI and robotics installation

Chen Baoyang works at the intersection of art and technology, treating AI and robotics not merely as tools but as collaborators. His installations give computational systems a physical presence, making machine behaviour perceptible through movement and space. I’ve Seen You Cry in the Rain extends this practice into affective territory, exploring how machines might register and respond to human emotion — or fail to. The work asks whether an AI can truly “see” a tear in the rain, or whether that metaphor belongs only to us.

ArtistChen Baoyang (b. 1989, Hangzhou) is an artist and curator exploring AI and robotics as creative collaborators.

DOKU the Self

Lu Yang · 2022 · Single-channel video, 36 min · Digital avatar, motion capture, real-time rendering

The DOKU series gives the artist a digital double that can be rendered, animated and handed to other people. It raises a plain question: what stays yours once your image becomes software? DOKU the Self is Lu Yang’s first narrative film in the ongoing DOKU series, premiered at the 59th Venice Biennale. Following a near-death experience in an airplane crash, DOKU journeys through six reincarnations from vengeful shaman to terrified passenger, cycling through death and rebirth in a journey through Buddhist cosmology toward the dissolution of essence.

ArtistLu Yang (b. 1984, Shanghai) is an artist whose work draws on neuroscience, Buddhist imagery, and game culture.
